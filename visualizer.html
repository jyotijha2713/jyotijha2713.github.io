The four rules

Every cell looks at its 8 neighbours, all at once, every generation:

  1. A live cell with fewer than 2 live neighbours dies (lonely).
  2. A live cell with 2 or 3 live neighbours survives.
  3. A live cell with more than 3 live neighbours dies (crowded).
  4. A dead cell with exactly 3 live neighbours is born.

That's the whole thing. Gliders that walk, patterns that blink, even a gun that fires gliders all come out of these four lines. Simple pieces, complex behaviour. That's what "simplifying the complexities" looks like. 🧩

Try this

  1. Load a Glider and press Play. It walks across the grid by itself.
  2. Load the R-pentomino. Just 5 cells, and it goes wild.
  3. Load the Glider gun and watch it fire gliders forever. (Wrap-around is off for this one, so the gliders fly away.)
  4. Clear the grid and draw your own shape. Can you make something that moves?

Peek inside 🔧

When you're ready, open visualizer.html in a text editor and look near the top of the script for these two lines:

var SURVIVE = [2, 3];
var BORN = [3];

They are the rules above, written as numbers. Try changing BORN to [3, 6]. That's a real variant called HighLife, and it has a pattern that copies itself. Re-upload the file to GitHub and your change goes live. That's your second commit, and your first real edit.
